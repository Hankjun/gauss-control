Common Heat Treatment Defects: Tracing Causes to Atmosphere and Temperature

Quick answer

Most heat treatment defects can be traced back to five reasons: temperature, atmosphere, time, quenching and furnace loading. This article summarizes the phenomena, possible causes and countermeasures of common defects in table form: for example, insufficient surface hardness is mostly related to decarburization or low carbon potential; network carbides are mostly caused by too high carbon potential or insufficient diffusion; layer depth fluctuations are often caused by carbon potential or temperature out of control; deformation and cracking are related to furnace loading, quenching intensity and materials.

  • Checking sequence: temperature → atmosphere → time → quenching → furnace loading → material
  • Low surface hardness, soft spots: common in decarburization, low carbon potential, and insufficient quenching
  • Excessive network carbides and paralympics: too high carbon potential or insufficient diffusion
  • Fluctuation of layer depth: unstable carbon potential/temperature control
  • Internal oxidation: selective oxidation of oxidizing components in the atmosphere, Cr/Mn/Si

Common Defects Comparison Table

Insufficient surface hardness Hardness is lower than the lower limit Decarburization, low carbon potential, insufficient quenching cooling rate, and high tempering temperature Calibrate carbon potential, check quenching medium and temperature
Soft spot Low local hardness Too dense furnace loading, insufficient stirring of quenching medium, and dirt on the surface of parts Optimize furnace loading, improve mixing and cleaning
Insufficient/too deep layer depth Effective hardened layer is out of tolerance Temperature deviation, carbon potential deviation, improper time TUS/SAT, carbon potential calibration, diffusion simulation

mesh carbide Surface layer carbides along grain boundaries Carbon potential is too high, insufficient diffusion, slow cooling Reduce strong carburization potential and extend diffusion
Too much retained austenite Low hardness and unstable dimensions Surface carbon/nitrogen is too high and quenching temperature is too high Reduce carbon potential/ammonia content, cold treatment

Suggestions for troubleshooting order

  1. temperature: Whether TUS, SAT and furnace temperature records are normal;
  2. atmosphere: Carbon potential/nitriding potential curve, calibration records, probe status, carrier gas composition;
  3. time: Whether each period of time is executed according to the process, and whether the door opening recovery is abnormal;
  4. Quenching: Medium temperature, stirring, transfer time, medium aging;
  5. Loading the furnace: Loading capacity, spacing, and parts placement;
  6. Material: Composition, initial microstructure, surface state.

Prevention is better than detection

  • Establish continuous monitoring and alarming of key parameters;
  • Use data trends to find early problems such as probe aging and generator drift;
  • Conduct FMEA and sample verification before new parts and new processes are put online;
  • Quality issues are formed into a case library and reviewed regularly.

Frequently asked questions

How to judge whether decarburization or insufficient quenching results in low hardness?

Do metallography: the decarburized surface layer contains ferrite or a structure with reduced carbon content; insufficient quenching shows a non-martensitic structure (such as troostite), and the surface carbon content is normal.

Can internal oxidation be completely avoided?

It is difficult to completely avoid it in an endothermic atmosphere containing H₂O and CO₂, but the depth can be reduced by controlling the oxidation of the atmosphere and selecting appropriate materials; low-pressure vacuum carburizing can basically eliminate it.

Can defective parts be reworked?

Parts can, for example, decarburization can be recarburized, and if the hardness is insufficient, it can be re-quenched. However, rework will increase deformation and cost, and must be reviewed and approved according to customer requirements.
