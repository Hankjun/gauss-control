Flowmeters in Atmosphere Control: Selection, Installation, and Troubleshooting

Quick answer

Flow is the "execution end" of atmosphere control: carrier gas flow maintains furnace pressure and ventilation, enriched gas/dilution air flow adjusts carbon potential, and ammonia and diluent gas flow adjusts nitriding potential. Commonly used flowmeters include rotameter (intuitive and cheap), thermal mass flowmeter (directly measures mass flow and can be transmitted remotely) and differential pressure flowmeter. The selection should consider the gas type, turndown ratio, pressure and temperature compensation and whether closed-loop control is required.

  • Carrier gas flow: maintain furnace pressure, ventilation, and prevent air infiltration
  • Enriched gas/dilution air: execution amount of carbon potential closed loop
  • Ammonia/diluent gas: Execution amount of nitriding potential closed loop
  • Rotameter is intuitive; thermal mass flowmeter is suitable for automatic control and recording
  • Different gas types require corresponding calibration or conversion coefficients

Common flow meter types

Type Principle Advantage Limitations
Rotameter The float rises and falls in the tapered tube with the flow Intuitive, cheap, no power required Need to be converted according to gas and pressure; difficult to record automatically (optional with remote transmission)
Thermal mass flow meter The heat taken away by the gas is related to the mass flow rate Direct measurement of mass flow, large range ratio, remote transmission and closed loop Need to be calibrated for gas components
Differential pressure type Pressure difference before and after the throttling part Suitable for large traffic Requires temperature and pressure compensation
Mass flow controller (MFC) Thermal measurement + built-in regulating valve Integrated measurement and control, high precision higher cost

Key points for selection

  1. Gas type: Different gases have different densities and thermal properties, and the flowmeter needs to be calibrated for the gas or use a conversion factor;
  2. Range and range ratio: Covering the minimum to maximum flow rate, enriched gas often works in the small flow section;
  3. pressure and temperature: The rotameter reading is affected by the working pressure and needs to be converted according to the actual pressure;
  4. Signals and Communications: When closed loop or recording is required, choose a model with analog or digital communication output;
  5. Safety: For flammable and toxic gases, explosion-proof and material compatibility must be considered.

Installation and maintenance

  • Leave enough straight pipe sections at the front and rear (according to the manufacturer's requirements);
  • A filter is installed upstream to prevent particles and oil contamination;
  • Rotameter is installed vertically;
  • Regularly check for leaks, zero point and reading accuracy;
  • Record the flow rate to identify anomalies (for example, a sudden drop in carrier gas flow rate may indicate a generator or piping problem).

Frequently asked questions

Are rotameter readings accurate?

It is more accurate under calibration conditions (gas, pressure, temperature); conversion is required when conditions change. When used for metering or closed-loop control, it is recommended to choose a mass flow meter.

What does the sudden increase in enriched gas flow mean?

The controller increases enriched gas in order to maintain the carbon potential. This may be due to furnace leakage, carrier gas quality degradation, low probe signal, or increased load. It should be investigated in conjunction with other data.

How to measure the amount of methanol dripped?

Liquid methanol usually uses a liquid flow meter or metering pump and is converted into gas volume (1 L of liquid methanol is approximately cracked into 1.66 m³ gas).
