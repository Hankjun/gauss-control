Load Tracking and Batch Traceability in Batch and Continuous Furnaces

Quick answer

The purpose of charge tracking is to know "at what time and what process conditions each part has experienced." Periodic furnaces use furnaces as units, and the batches can be bound to the furnaces; parts in continuous furnaces (mesh belt furnaces, pusher furnaces) are constantly flowing, and the residence time of each batch of parts in each zone needs to be calculated based on the charging time, conveying speed or tray position, and then corresponds to the process data. Barcodes or RFID reduce manual entry errors.

  • Goal: Part → Time → Accurate correspondence to process conditions
  • Periodic furnace: batch binding furnace
  • Continuous furnace: modeled by charging time, belt speed/push cycle or tray position
  • Barcode/RFID reduces entry errors
  • In the event of an abnormality, the affected batches can be accurately identified to reduce the isolation scope.

Why you need charge tracking

When the furnace temperature or carbon potential is abnormal during a certain period of time, two questions must be answered:

  1. Which parts are affected?
  2. Are other parts safe?

If you cannot answer accurately, you can only expand the scope of isolation, resulting in a large number of returns for inspection or even scrapping.

Periodic furnace (integral-quench furnace, pit furnace)

In terms of heats:

  1. Scan or enter part batches when loading the furnace;
  2. The system generates the furnace number and binds it to the process card and equipment;
  3. Heat curves are automatically associated;
  4. The steps of releasing, tempering and inspection continue to be bound to the same batch.

The integral-quench furnace production line also needs to track the flow of material baskets between cleaning, heating, quenching, and tempering equipment.

Continuous furnace (mesh belt furnace, pusher furnace, roller hearth furnace)

Parts are constantly coming in and leaving, there is no natural “batch.” Commonly used methods:

Method Description Applicable
time window method Record the start and end time of loading each batch of parts, and calculate the time in each zone based on the belt speed. Mesh belt furnace
Tray position method Each tray number is tracked according to the pushing rhythm of its section. pusher furnace, roller hearth furnace
partition mark Place partitions or gaps between different batches Small batches and multiple varieties

After calculating the time of each batch of parts in each section, the corresponding time period can be intercepted from the process data to generate a "batch-level" process curve.

Barcodes and RFID

  • barcode: Low cost, suitable for baskets, trays and accompanying cards;
  • RFID: High temperature resistant labels can be used on material trays to automatically identify and reduce labor;
  • The key is to force scanning and confirmation at key points such as loading and unloading.

Value in exception handling

When the system finds that the carbon potential is out of tolerance for a certain period of time:

  1. Automatically calculate the batches in this area during this time period;
  2. Freeze relevant batches and notify the quality department;
  3. The remaining batches will be released normally.

This not only ensures quality, but also avoids "one size fits all" large-scale isolation.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if the belt speed of the mesh belt furnace changes?

The tracking model should read the belt speed in real time rather than using a fixed value, otherwise the residence time calculation will be biased.

Can mixed batch furnace loading be traced?

Yes, but the position or time period of each batch on the basket or mesh belt must be recorded when loading the furnace, otherwise it can only be traced according to the entire furnace.

How long should traceability data be kept?

According to customer and system requirements, automotive parts usually require a longer shelf life.
