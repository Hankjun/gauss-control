Carburizing and Carbon Potential Control

How Oxygen Probes Work: Zirconia, Reference Air, and the Nernst Equation

Quick answer

The core of the oxygen probe is a closed zirconia ceramic tube. Zirconia can conduct oxygen ions at high temperatures. When the oxygen partial pressure inside the tube (reference air, containing 20.9% oxygen) is different from that outside the tube (furnace gas), an electromotive force (mV) is generated between the inner and outer electrodes. According to the Nernst equation E = 0.0496·T·lg(0.209/pO₂) (E is in mV, T is the temperature in Kelvin), the oxygen partial pressure of the furnace gas can be calculated from mV and temperature, and then combined with CO% to calculate the carbon potential.

  • Zirconia conducts oxygen ions at high temperatures to form an oxygen concentration difference battery
  • Reference air (20.9% O₂) is passed through the tube, and the outside of the tube is exposed to furnace gas.
  • Nernst's equation relates mV, temperature, and oxygen partial pressure
  • Carbon potential = f (mV, temperature, CO%)
  • With in-situ measurement and fast response, it is the mainstream sensor for carbon potential control in the furnace.

Structure

A typical oxygen probe consists of the following parts:

  • Zirconia sensing element: Located at the front end of the probe, exposed to high-temperature furnace gas;
  • internal electrode: Usually platinum, contacting the reference air in the tube;
  • external electrode: Heat-resistant metal outer tube or electrode, in contact with furnace gas;
  • Reference air channel: Clean air is continuously supplied by a small air pump;
  • Thermocouple: Measure probe tip temperature (some probes are built-in);
  • Charcoal air connection: Used to regularly remove carbon on the surface of the probe.

Common zirconia structures on the market include integral zirconia tubes, zirconium balls, zirconia and ceramic welding, and zirconia tips. No matter which type, good sealing between the zirconia and the ceramic inner tube is a prerequisite for accuracy.

Working principle: Oxygen concentration difference battery

Zirconia (stabilized by doping) allows the passage of oxygen ions at high temperatures. When the oxygen partial pressure inside and outside the tube is different, oxygen ions migrate from the side with high oxygen partial pressure to the side with low oxygen partial pressure, forming a potential difference between the two poles.

The oxygen partial pressure of the carburizing atmosphere in the furnace is extremely low (usually in the order of 10⁻¹⁵ bar or even lower), whereas the reference air contains 20.9% oxygen, thus producing a signal of the order of approximately 1 V.

Nernst equation

E = (RT / 4F) · ln(pO₂,ref / pO₂,gas)

Substituting constants and calculating with reference air pO₂,ref = 0.209 bar, it is often written as:

E (mV) = 0.0496 × T × lg(0.209 / pO₂)

Where T is the probe tip temperature (K), pO₂ is the oxygen partial pressure of the furnace gas (bar).

From mV to carbon potential

In an equilibrium atmosphere with a known CO content, the oxygen partial pressure is related to the CO₂/CO ratio (2CO + O₂ ⇌ 2CO₂), which in turn is related to the carbon activity. The controller therefore uses three inputs to calculate the carbon potential:

  1. Probe mV;
  2. temperature;
  3. CO% (set value or actual measured value).

At the same temperature and CO%,The higher the mV, the higher the carbon potential. Signals in carburizing atmospheres are typically in the 1100–1200 mV range, depending on temperature and carbon potential settings.

Why oxygen probes are suitable for closed-loop control

  • Directly inserted into the furnace, no sampling required, short response time;
  • The signal is continuous, suitable for PID closed-loop adjustment of enriched gas or diluted air;
  • The cost is relatively low and it is one of the most effective and economical sensors for carbon potential control.

Frequently asked questions

Why must reference air be clean and stable?

The Nernst equation assumes that the reference side oxygen partial pressure is constant. Mixing oil, water or insufficient flow in the reference air will cause changes in the reference oxygen partial pressure and signal distortion.

What does it mean if the probe signal is lower than the normal value?

It may be due to probe aging, sealing failure, carbon deposits, poor electrode contact or reference air problems, or it may be a decrease in the true carbon potential in the furnace. Cross-validation with an analyzer or carbon tablet is required.

What is the furnace temperature below which the oxygen probe cannot be used?

Zirconia requires a high enough temperature to conduct electricity. Reliable signals can generally be obtained above about 650–700 °C, please refer to the probe manual for details.
