Capabilities and Limits of Carburizing Simulation Software

Quick answer

The carburizing simulation software is based on the diffusion model, calculates carbon concentration distribution based on material composition, temperature and carbon potential curve, predicts surface carbon and layer depth, and can be used for process development, parameter optimization, training and anomaly analysis. It cannot replace metallographic and hardness inspection. Its accuracy depends on model parameter calibration and input data quality. It cannot directly predict defects caused by deformation, cracking and non-atmosphere factors.

  • Can do: carbon concentration curve, layer depth prediction, optimization of strong penetration diffusion time, process comparison
  • Can do: Anomaly analysis - assess the impact of temperature or carbon potential deviations on results
  • Cannot do: Substitute inspection, direct prediction of deformation and cracking
  • Accuracy dependent: material data, carbon transfer coefficient, temperature input, calibration
  • Best use: Simulation + small amount of validation testing

What can be done

1. Process development Input the target layer depth and surface carbon, and the software calculates strong penetration, diffusion time and carbon potential settings, reducing the number of trials and errors.

2. Process optimization Compare the total time and results of different temperature and carbon potential combinations to find solutions to save energy or increase production capacity.

3. Anomaly analysis When there is a carbon potential or temperature deviation in a furnace, the actual curve is input into the model to evaluate the impact on layer depth and surface carbon to help determine whether the part can be released or needs to be inspected.

4. Training Visually display the changes in carbon concentration over time to help new employees understand the principles of carburization.

5. Online calculation (combined with controller) Input real-time data into the model, dynamically estimate the layer depth, and end carburization when the target is reached.

What input is required

  • material chemical composition;
  • Initial carbon content;
  • Temperature-time curve;
  • Carbon potential-time curve;
  • Atmosphere type (carbon transfer coefficient);
  • Part section or location of interest (simple models are usually calculated in one dimension on the surface).

What not to do

  • Not a substitute for inspection: The final quality judgment still requires metallography and hardness;
  • Deformation and cracking cannot be directly predicted: This requires more complex simulations that take into account phase transitions, stresses, and geometry;
  • Cannot compensate for incorrect input: The temperature measurement is inaccurate, the carbon potential display is biased, and the simulation results will also be biased;
  • Not all defects can be covered: Internal oxidation, soft spots caused by furnace loading, etc. are not within the scope of the diffusion model.

How to improve credibility

  1. Calibrate model parameters using actual measurement results of representative parts;
  2. Ensure temperature and carbon potential data are accurate (TUS, SAT, carbon potential calibration);
  3. Verify separately for different furnace types;
  4. Record the deviation between simulation and actual measurement and make continuous corrections.

Frequently asked questions

What is the normal deviation between simulation and actual measurement?

Depends on model and data quality. The key is that the deviation is stable and interpretable; if the deviation is large or unstable, the input data and model parameters should be checked.

Can carbonitriding be simulated?

Some software supports carbonitriding, but it requires nitrogen-related diffusion parameters and atmosphere data, making it more complex. You should confirm whether the software supports it.

Does simulation software require professionals to operate it?

The basic operation is not difficult, but setting parameters and interpreting results require craftsmen who understand the principles of carburizing. It is recommended that the process engineer be responsible.
